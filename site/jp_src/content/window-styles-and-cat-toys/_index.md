<div class="row g-5 mb-2">
<div class="col-12 col-md-4">
<h5 class="sg-indigo-500 fw-semibold mb-3">The Problem</h5>
<p class="text-muted mb-3">SmartGit offers three <a href="/features/three-window-styles/">window styles</a> — <strong>Standard</strong>, <strong>Log</strong>, and <strong>Working Tree</strong>. They look quite similar, so it's easy to lose track of which one you're in.</p>
<p class="text-muted mb-0">This is the <strong>number one confusion</strong> we hear in user feedback — we've changed the default window style over time, and many users simply step over the setup without noticing.</p>
</div>
<div class="col-12 col-md-4">
<h5 class="sg-indigo-500 fw-semibold mb-3">The Idea</h5>
<p class="text-muted mb-3">Instead of relying on names or descriptions, each window has a distinct <strong>visual identity</strong> — a unique <strong>icon</strong> and a consistent <strong>color</strong>.</p>
<p class="text-muted mb-0">The icons are intentionally unrelated to Git, which makes them easier to recognize and remember. Just check the icon in the <strong>top right corner</strong> and you instantly know which window you're in.</p>
</div>
<div class="col-12 col-md-4">
<h5 class="sg-indigo-500 fw-semibold mb-3">Why It Works</h5>
<p class="text-muted mb-3">Humans are much better at <strong>recognizing shapes and colors</strong> than remembering names. These icons take advantage of that.</p>
<p class="text-muted mb-0">It may feel unusual at first. But once you get used to it, it becomes a <strong>simple and reliable</strong> way to orient yourself in SmartGit.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="col-12 col-lg-7 ps-lg-5">
<h3 class="mb-3">Why Cat Toys?</h3>
<p class="text-muted mb-3">SmartGit has a mascot: the <strong><a href="https://www.smartgitty.com/#!/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">SmartGitty</a></strong>. The icons are objects from the cat's world, which makes them <strong>simple, visually distinct, and easy to remember</strong>.</p>
<ul class="text-muted mb-0 ps-3">
<li class="mb-2"><strong>Easy to spot:</strong> a ball of yarn, a toy mouse, and a feather are easier to tell apart at a glance than three similar-looking windows.</li>
<li><strong>Easy to remember:</strong> after a short time, you stop thinking in window names and just recognize <strong>the blue yarn window</strong> or <strong>the orange mouse window</strong>.</li>
</ul>
